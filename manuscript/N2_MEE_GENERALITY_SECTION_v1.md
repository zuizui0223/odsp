## Methods insert — Generality and invariance validation

To distinguish a method that merely accepts several named data types from one whose estimands are genuinely axis-agnostic, we defined a set of analytic invariance and composition properties before interpreting the empirical applications. For a finite non-negative support tensor with declared base axes `B` and added axes `A`, normalization implies invariance of `H(A|B)` and `I(A;B)` to positive rescaling of total support mass. Because entropy and mutual information are invariant under bijective state relabelling, the same quantities must also be invariant to category permutations and equivariant to tensor-axis permutation when the declared axis indices are remapped accordingly. If an undeclared nuisance axis only refines state masses so that marginalizing it recovers the original `P(A,B)`, the evidence quantities must remain unchanged. Under evaluation on the same generating distribution, the conditional-versus-marginal held-out score has the exact identity `E[log P(A|B)-log P(A)] = I(A;B)`. Finally, if two added dimensions are conditionally independent given the base, conditional entropy is additive and effective state counts are multiplicative.

We converted these identities into a deterministic property benchmark rather than validating only hand-selected `x-y-z` or site-time examples. With seed `20260904`, the benchmark generated 128 heterogeneous positive support tensors spanning two to six dimensions, varying state cardinalities from two to five and placing one or two base axes and one or two added axes at arbitrary tensor positions. Every tensor was subjected to independent positive model/test rescaling, axis permutation, state-label permutation and an omitted nuisance-axis refinement. The benchmark also generated 32 random conditionally independent two-added-axis systems with two to six states per axis. Independent-group logic was tested with one, three and seven groups and with group masses differing by many orders of magnitude. The complete benchmark contains 1,873 deterministic proof obligations and fails if any identity exceeds its fixed floating-point tolerance. This validation concerns the mathematical and implementation domain of finite discrete support tensors; it does not validate the biological meaning of a chosen axis or remove observation bias.

## Results insert — The evidence core was invariant across high-dimensional representations

The deterministic generality benchmark passed all **1,873 of 1,873** proof obligations, with a maximum absolute numerical error of `2.4868995751603507e-14`. Across the 128 random two- to six-dimensional tensors, same-distribution held-out gain equalled fitted `I(A;B)` regardless of axis position or whether `B` and `A` each contained one or multiple axes. Positive rescaling of model and held-out support, arbitrary tensor-axis permutations, arbitrary category relabelling and addition of a marginalized nuisance/refinement axis left conditional information, fitted organization and held-out gain unchanged within floating-point tolerance. Effective state counts always remained between one and the joint cardinality of the declared added state. In the random conditionally independent systems, joint conditional information equalled the sum of the component conditional informations and joint effective state count equalled their product. Group-level gains and grouped terminal classification were unchanged when group masses were rescaled, and same-process results remained generalizing with one, three or seven independent groups.

These tests extend the known-truth examples from particular vertical and temporal shapes to arbitrary finite base-added axis partitions. The generic API therefore does not require an axis to be designated as height or time: the same estimator can be applied to, for example, behaviour, substrate or microhabitat states provided their observation semantics and independent validation design are declared prospectively.

## Discussion insert — What kind of generality is supported

The evidence for generality has two distinct levels. First, the core estimands have representation-level generality over finite discrete support tensors. Their invariance to positive mass, axis order and category labels, the exact identity between same-distribution transfer gain and mutual information, and the composition rule for conditionally independent added dimensions follow from standard probability and information identities and were independently exercised across a broad randomized implementation space. This is stronger than demonstrating that the same code happened to run on a bat and a camera-trap data set.

Second, empirical portability is supported across heterogeneous observation architectures. The Tawaki lane used linked GPS and dive records with a site-year structural gate, the bat lane used same-event three-dimensional GPS with whole-individual validation, and the Serengeti lane used camera detections with effort weighting and cross-fitted site folds. These applications differed in taxon scope, measurement process, added-axis meaning and independent replication unit, yet the same estimability-thickness-organization-transferability hierarchy produced coherent terminal states. The empirical set therefore demonstrates portability of the inferential machinery, not a universal biological frequency of those states.

This distinction defines the claim ceiling. We do not infer that all ecological niches are thick, that most thick structure transfers, that temporal dimensions are intrinsically more generalizable than vertical dimensions, or that discretization is optimal for every continuous variable. Nor do the information-theoretic identities solve detectability, representativeness or causal-identification problems. The general contribution is instead a portable evidence architecture: once an ecological state representation and independent validation unit are scientifically justified, the same inferential logic can determine whether the added state is estimable, how much information it contributes beyond the retained representation, whether that information is organized, and whether the organization survives independent transfer.
