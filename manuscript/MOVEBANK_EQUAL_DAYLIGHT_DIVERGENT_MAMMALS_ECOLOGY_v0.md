# Equal daylight, divergent mammalian movement
## A source-verified public-telemetry comparison of seasonal diel displacement across six cohorts

**Ecology manuscript candidate v0.5 — 2026-10-23. Exploratory observational Results, not yet submission-ready.**  
**DO NOT merge raw camera photographs, animal movement, inferred continuous activity or official animal source coordinates into one endpoint.**

### Abstract

Daylength is a powerful organizing signal for animal temporal activity, but similar hours of sunlight on opposite annual seasonal branches need not coincide with the same weather, resources or internal biological state. We constructed a provenance-tracked comparison from the complete **437-package public Movebank Data Repository discovery frame**, resolved its **1,677 native ORIGINAL file entries** across all fixed package IDs, and analyzed original checksum-confirmed GPS trajectories in a prospectively fixed three-source pilot followed by an explicitly exploratory eight-source extension. The same observation rule used individual-specific paired dates separated by at least 30 days and matching modeled photoperiod within five minutes, adjacent original GPS locations at 30–120-minute sampling intervals, and adequate observed daylight and night sampling on both branches. The registered response was falling-minus-rising `[log(1+median night minimum straight-line displacement km/h) − log(1+median day displacement km/h)]`, with individual and annual repeated measures aggregated before source-level uncertainty assessment.

Six study cohorts from the initially tested eleven native source packages yielded **197 eligible individual animals**. The source-observed mean diel displacement response was positive among 12 Canadian wapiti (+0.0450, source-individual bootstrap 95% percentile [+0.0241,+0.0619]) and 118 Wyoming mule deer (+0.0311, [+0.0247,+0.0371]), negative among 20 Connecticut black bears (−0.0772, [−0.0888,−0.0659]), approximately null in five Florida wild pigs (−0.0010, [−0.0401,+0.0335]) and 33 Virginia white-tailed deer (−0.0032, [−0.0092,+0.0043]), and inconclusive among nine Alberta wolves (+0.0284, [−0.0960,+0.1862]). The two cervid positive responses each combined less recorded daylight displacement and greater nighttime displacement, whereas the bear response was dominated by more daylight displacement. All six source populations were subsequently linked *post-outcome* to ERA5 at their actual source-selected matched GPS dates, without refitting earlier movement effects. Declining-daylight regional mean-air-temperature contrasts were positive in all six original-source cohorts (+1.79 to +9.87°C), but GPS displacement responses varied in sign and magnitude. Particularly, Wyoming mule deer (+9.27°C; antecedent 30-day regional P−ET0 −82.6 mm) showed a positive relative NIGHT shift (+0.03115 log1p km/h), whereas Connecticut black bears (+9.87°C; P−ET0 −66.5 mm) showed a negative one (−0.07717). Exploratory within-individual-year contrasts additionally showed heterogeneous regional thermal co-trends (+0.00339 per °C in Wyoming mule deer versus −0.00290 in Connecticut black bears), while Virginia white-tailed deer had a near-zero group seasonal mean yet positive original-date temperature slopes in 31/33 sampled animals. These shared-calendar associations are not independent climate treatments or heat-avoidance evidence because taxon, location, seasonal state and tag sampling are confounded. A single fixed sensitivity adjusting each individual-year climate/movement association for its matched pair's distance from the June solstice reversed the apparent Connecticut black-bear regional-temperature slope (−0.00290 to +0.00109) and markedly altered the daytime/nighttime components of the Wyoming mule-deer relationship, demonstrating a major calendar-interpretation limit rather than independently confirming a thermal mechanism.

A source-preserving temperature-association analysis of the same six cohorts further showed that the positive relative NIGHT−DAY slope for Wyoming mule deer and Virginia deer was associated mainly with a **negative DAY** GPS component, whereas the Connecticut black bear negative relative slope accompanied **positive DAY and NIGHT** components with DAY stronger. The results demonstrate heterogeneous observed seasonal diel displacement at approximately equal daylength **within the source-selected North American tracking cohorts**; they do not identify direct light entrainment, latent 24-hour animal activity budgets, causal heat avoidance, species-wide patterns or globally representative response frequencies. Future independent population cohorts with source-verified duty cycles, used-location thermal and phenology exposures, and within-species replication are essential to explain the heterogeneity.

**Keywords:** seasonal ecology; mammal telemetry; photoperiod; diel movement; environmental calendars; seasonal plasticity; public biological databases; sampling effort.

### Introduction

The daily alternation of light and darkness constrains opportunities for animal movement, while temperature, vegetation, food supply, reproductive state and human exposure vary over the longer seasonal calendar. Solar daylength repeats on two dates on opposite sides of a solstice, often under different ecological conditions. This makes the two calendar branches a physically motivated setting for examining the seasonal organization of recorded movement, independently of whether animals respond to astronomical daylength itself.

A simple prediction of photoperiod-only timing would be that an individual's daytime and nighttime displacement allocation is similar when nominal daylength is held close. Competing ecological explanations predict that environmental history and life stage can lead to differences. Those differences need not share one sign across taxa: a summer heat-sensitive population might reduce midday travel; a predator's spatial sampling or reproductive ecology could produce quite different changes; shifting ranges can alter both ambient exposure and its own observed time niche.

Seasonality and solar adjustment are not new methods or biological discoveries. Prior research has already shown mammalian season-dependent day/night activity and documented state-dependent photoperiod hysteresis in laboratory and field contexts. The question addressed here is **the empirical heterogeneity of same-individual GPS displacement contrasts under a fixed astronomy/effort filter across independently published terrestrial mammal tracking archives**, not a novel method for representing a clock.

### Public source selection and original-data provenance

A public archive query captured the **full 437-package 2026-10-19 Movebank Data Repository 'Data packages' collection**, not a collection of 437 independent wild mammal populations. Native ORIGINAL bitstream metadata were resolved for every package (**437/437**, **1,677** original bitstream entries including non-animal files) with original checksum and license provenance. The first source-only within-animal solar-date suitability pilot named three North American ungulate/omnivore/carnivore archives before opening movement values, then a separate expansion named eight additional source packages from the same frame after the initial ecological results were already known. The present six-source Results are therefore exploratory even though the *within-source original outcome rules* were not tuned to effect signs.

Every original trajectory used in the six analyzable cohorts was matched to its public provider ORIGINAL file UUID and provider-stored MD5. Four of the eleven originally named sources were retained as **SOURCE HOLD** because their returned bytes did not match stored MD5; one moose cohort had insufficient matched date-pair support. Neither source failure nor nonqualification was treated as a zero animal response. The six analyzed cohorts below were NOT drawn as a random subset of the world's mammalian species.

### Movement and astronomic comparison

Original UTC individual locations were time sorted, and the valid sampling unit was an original consecutive GPS position interval **30–120 minutes** long, fully classified as a single solar DAY or NIGHT within the same local mean-solar calendar date by both ends and midpoint. Great-circle displacement divided by elapsed time is a **minimum straight-line observed km/h**, not the animal's actual path length, energetics or locomotor activity time. A step was excluded if its source speed was not finite or exceeded the fixed plausibility threshold 60 km/h.

A source individual×year needed at least three valid displacement steps in each daypart on each candidate date. Pre/post June solstice dates in the Northern Hemisphere were then paired 1:1 on approximate daylength difference **≤5 minutes**, at least 30 calendar days apart, with day and night eligible step-count ratios across branches in **[0.5,2]**. Every source individual-year required **≥10** such paired dates. All date selection was source astronomy and observation quality based, never movement-effect based. Each animal's paired contrasts were averaged within year, then among available years, then individuals were weighted equally in cohort summaries. Individual-bootstrap percentile intervals (2,000 deterministic resamples) describe conditional sampled individuals, **not taxon-general or independent-population confidence intervals**.

All original GPS identifiers, exact fixes and individual effects remain restricted to ephemeral source-audited processing; source reports contain grouped provenance, counts, result effects, signs and uncertainty only.

### Results 4 — within individual, date-pair hydrothermal associations differ despite shared solar geometry

A cohort-level six-point climate comparison cannot identify an ecological mechanism. To improve ecological resolution without changing the original observations, we froze a separate **post-original-outcome** within-animal-year paired-date slope question. Every one of the six original MD5-authenticated Movebank sources, its exact original cohort GPS mean and eligible-animal count was verified **before** acquiring date-paired ERA5 climate values. All original 0.5–2-hour straight-line GPS steps, sampling-balance conditions and matched daylength dates were reused unchanged. [First original source replay CI 39217223363](https://github.com/zuizui0223/odsp/actions/runs/39217223363) completed six tests and retained all **197 original marked individuals** with **zero missing regional ERA5 weather date pairs**.

For each original marked animal-year, the already frozen GPS *falling-minus-rising NIGHT-minus-DAY* displacement contrast on each original matched date pair was regressed descriptively against that pair's ERA5 regional mean-air-temperature difference. Each individual-year was required to have at least 10 original qualified pairs and an SD of temperature contrast above 0.5°C; slopes were averaged across source years *within each marked animal*, then original animals weighted equally within source. These are observational univariate time-series co-trends, **not** causal thermal regulation or individual microclimate measurements.

**White-tailed deer illustrates the biologically important distinction between a seasonal mean and a conditional observation pattern.** Their overall source seasonal NIGHT-minus-DAY change was approximately zero, yet the original date-pair *regional temperature contrast* slope was positive in 31/33 source-admitted animals. That is not 31 independent heat experiments: tagged animals within an original source shared regional ERA5 values and overlapping dates, so a common underlying seasonal trend or GPS measurement regime may produce correlated animal slopes. Similarly, Wyoming mule deer and Connecticut black bear mean slopes differed in sign (+0.00339 versus −0.00290 per °C) although their mean regional declining-daylength season was warmer/drier in both cohorts. The source-specific covariation is interesting but cannot distinguish taxa from location, mating/migration stage, collar scheduling, season phase or cover ecology.

A separately frozen *univariate* regional previous-30-day P−ET0 slope, per +100mm, was **−0.07213** for Wyoming mule deer (102/118 negative individuals), **+0.05657** for Connecticut black bear (16/20 positive), and **−0.01674** for Virginia deer (24/33 negative). T and P−ET0 are correlated environment proxies in the same ERA5 regional model and **were not simultaneously adjusted**; there are no causal mediation or species population p-value claims.

This result creates a concrete ecological test for future independent tracked populations: do individual-matched hydrothermal differences correspond to daytime reduction, nighttime increase or both when source timing/trait/habitat are harmonized? The existing six-source pairwise slopes do **not** prove that organisms respond to those environmental factors, that daylight is their sole temporal cue, or that ecological-season memory is universal. ### Results 5 — warmer-date displacement responses can reflect daytime suppression, not nighttime enhancement

The previous six-cohort within-original-animal-year regional ERA5 temperature association was defined on **relative** NIGHT-minus-DAY GPS displacement, without separately showing its DAY and NIGHT effects. This distinction is biologically important: positive relative nightward movement does **not** imply an actual increase in nighttime displacement, and a negative relative contrast need not imply absolute nighttime avoidance.

We therefore conducted a separately frozen **post-original-outcome exploratory ecological decomposition** on the exact same six original Movebank native GPS sources (**197 qualifying marked individuals**) and their original individually matched near-equal-daylength dates. The [new first original-source replay CI 39722587356](https://github.com/zuizui0223/odsp/actions/runs/39722587356) completed all **five** known-truth tests, provider-original MD5 checks, unmodified `locked_pairs` six source-effect and sample count guards, and an independent line-by-line audit of identical original date pairs and relative GPS outcomes, BEFORE calculating the newly requested component effects. It reused the previously archived [canonical ERA5 scientific original daily arrays](https://github.com/zuizui0223/odsp/actions/runs/39220116497) from the original climate-source-v1 run (source snapshot SHA256 `8b637760b9199e6e7f9f85e647b74658827525ed42d400bc06d44c6a4a14f05e`), whose per-original-chunk scientific SHA256s, original daily units, provider grids and original matched-date source group means were all verified. No GPS source cohort, pair, observational effort requirement or previously reported relative movement/ERA5 effect was changed.

For every original animal×source calendar year with ≥10 previously qualified solar/day-night sample pairs and sufficient original regional temperature difference variation (SD>0.5°C), we fit **three observational OLS slopes against the SAME original falling-minus-rising paired-date modelled ERA5 temperature**: absolute observed DAY displacement change, absolute observed NIGHT displacement change and relative NIGHT-minus-DAY change. Each slope was averaged across original eligible years within individual and original individuals equally within source. By linear identity, `βrelative = βnight − βday`, both for each year and source means. Every previously published source-mean univariate relative-temperature and relative-water-balance slope was exactly reproduced (≤1e−9 numerical tolerance); six original source outcomes all remain admissible.

In **Wyoming mule deer**, the positive relative nightward temperature slope was driven predominantly by the **negative DAY** component (100/118 source animals showed negative individual-average DAY slopes), whereas the NIGHT component was positive but much smaller (60/118 individual-average NIGHT slopes positive). The same pattern was seen in **Virginia deer**: all **33/33** originally tagged animals had negative DAY temperature slopes, but only 21 had a positive NIGHT slope. Thus its almost-zero *season-mean* relative movement effect did not imply no source date-pair climatic covariation; the positive relative slope (+0.001474/°C) mainly reflects lower DAY displacement among the sampled warm-contrasted dates. In **Connecticut black bears**, both observed absolute source DAY (+0.003869/°C) and NIGHT (+0.000966/°C) displacement co-trends were positive, but DAY rose more strongly, resulting in a **negative** relative nightward thermal slope (−0.002903/°C). These are **four qualitatively different source-context component patterns** that can be hidden by simply naming the sign of the NIGHT-relative effect. The original two source elephant cohorts showed similarly that same-sign seasonal relative movement can have distinct absolute time-zone changes, but they are independent recruitment/time-source examples and are not pooled with this table.

Wolf water slopes were supported in only 7/9 original animals.

Although some original source animal sign counts strongly agree, those animals share weather dates and cannot be interpreted as independent ecological temperature manipulations. The result is a **post-outcome source-preserving component diagnostic** rather than independent external validation or a predictive universal law. ### Results 6 — the inferred climate association changes when season-phase distance is controlled

An important limitation of both the previous source-level thermal contrast and individual-year thermal co-trend is that the matched daylight geometry leaves a **within-year position on the annual seasonal calendar**: matched sunrise/daylength dates farther from June 21 may also differ in vegetation, reproduction, migration, temperature history and the original GPS displacement response. The initial original individual-year univariate OLS coefficients were explicitly exploratory and were NOT a causal estimate of an animal's direct thermal response.

We therefore performed one separately registered **2026-10-23, post-prior-outcome calendar-distance sensitivity**, with its linear adjustment and source/animal support thresholds fixed before opening the new residual outcomes. Every one of the **six original provider-MD5 GPS files and 197 original admitted individual animals**, original paired dates/absolute DAY/NIGHT values, prior regional ERA5 daily scientific arrays and first group slopes were independently source-replayed, with seven known-truth checks, in [GitHub Actions 39878137228](https://github.com/zuizui0223/odsp/actions/runs/39878137228). The previously frozen original results, source-HOLDs and independent sensor routes were not modified.

For each original June-solstice-straddling rising/falling date pair, we defined `k` as their **average absolute day distance from June 21**. Within each original individual and original calendar year, we residualized *both* original `ΔT` and source observed DAY, NIGHT and NIGHT−DAY movement differences against an **intercept and linear k**, then related residual original GPS difference to residual regional ΔT using the same original matched dates (FWL linear OLS). We required >=10 original date pairs, SD(k)>=7 days, >=3 unique k values and SD(residual ΔT)>0.5°C; unsupported years were marked undefined, never retuned. For fairness, the old unadjusted coefficient was recomputed on exactly those *same* source years/individuals and shown beside the new adjusted coefficient, so support differences do not manufacture a seasonal correction effect. The source group remains descriptive equal-year-then-equal-individual sampling, not climate experimental replication.

Within supported individuals `βREL=βNIGHT−βDAY` holds exactly. The source means are not population-general species parameters; for wild pigs and wolves the leave-one-individual-out descriptive mean range crosses zero. **The most important empirical diagnostic is in Connecticut black bears.** With exactly the same 20 source individuals, the originally negative relative-night temperature association **reverses sign** from −0.002903 to +0.001094 after only the fixed linear distance-from-solstice adjustment. Previously source βDAY was strongly positive (+0.003869) versus a smaller positive βNIGHT (+0.000966). After adjustment βDAY is essentially zero (−0.000077) while βNIGHT remains positive (+0.001016). This demonstrates that the apparent source “more daytime displacement with bigger matched-day heat difference” depends strongly on how annual calendar progression is represented. It does NOT prove that the true black-bear temperature effect is positive or that calendar phase itself causes the shift.

**Wyoming mule deer** retain a positive source βREL with all 118 originally qualified animals, but it shrinks from +0.003391 to +0.002103; its DAY component shifts from original **−0.002802** toward essentially zero **−0.000035**, while the NIGHT component becomes **+0.002067** (from +0.000590). Thus even when the signed *relative* movement association survives, the apparent biological **DAY versus NIGHT pathway changes** under calendar handling. **Virginia deer** retain positive relative slopes (+0.001601 after control, 32/33 individual estimates positive), but shared source/calendar weather and season remain unseparated beyond this linear k. A separate, fixed univariate `Δ30d(P−ET0)` calendar sensitivity attenuates the mule-deer negative source relative association from −0.07265 to −0.01519 per 100mm on its 117 independently supported source animals; this cannot be interpreted as water/forage mediation because T was not simultaneously controlled.

**Scientific boundary:** conditioning on k can remove genuine environmentally relevant seasonal signal as well as an incidental calendar co-trend; k does not capture non-linear phase, animal reproductive stage, regional vegetation history, source duty-cycle changes or individual operative temperature. All 197 animals share only six broad modelled regional weather reference points, so individual coefficient directions do not represent independent climate interventions. These source results **weaken causal “heat produces daytime suppression” interpretations** of the original within-year univariate βT, rather than establish a new positive universal nighttime thermal pattern. Full source, matched-support comparisons and limits: [calendar-phase sensitivity report](../docs/movebank_six_original_calendar_phase_confound_ecology_20261023.md), [frozen contract](../research/MOVEBANK_SIX_PHASE_ADJUSTED_GPS_ERA5_V0_20261023.json).

### Discussion — what generalizes and what does not

Within the source-selected mammalian telemetry cohorts, similar daily sunlit duration on opposite annual branches does not fix the observed relative day-night displacement outcome. There are repeatable individual-sign tendencies within certain sampled source cohorts, but the overall directions differ across the studied groups. The finding is **population-level observational heterogeneity**, and does not yet identify its shared environmental drivers or the population of all terrestrial mammals.

Two specific ecological pathways remain to be tested in independently sampled populations: (1) time-use plasticity related to experienced daytime thermal burden, which might be expressed as **less daytime displacement, more nighttime displacement or both**; (2) resource or water-season context that changes the animal's range usage and encounters, which could alter both temporal allocation and where sensors find animals. A source with a near-zero relative day/night difference may still have large, offsetting changes in both absolute day and night movement.

The independently studied Serengeti camera/NDVI results provide **motivation**, not a numeric replication of the GPS estimand: under same-daylength photo-date matching NASA/Terra MODIS greenness rose in the original quality-eligible source cameras, but original camera-hour uptime and detection `q` were unknown. A separate sparse 21-land-grid ERA5 1/15/30d climate pilot shows heterogeneous warm/dry versus cool/wet histories even at similar endpoint solar photoperiod. Neither external strand supplies new animal population effects in the six GPS cohorts; mixing them into a single animal meta-analysis would be scientifically unjustified.

**Critical counterfactuals.** Matching daylength does not match weather, resource phenology, reproductive stage or human exposure. The initial eleven sources were selected for published source data availability and animal count, not randomly across taxa/geographies. Source selection and GPS duty cycle could differ systematically across time, species and habitat. Individual bootstrap intervals under the original qualified-source roster do not correct this source-selection or detection bias. No raw event timestamps or precise coordinates are redistributed.

### Conclusion and immediate empirical next test

The first genuinely source-verified public mammal GPS comparison reveals **nonuniform seasonal diel displacement reallocation at near-matched solar daylength**, with particular source cohorts shifting in opposing directions. The ecological explanation remains unknown. The highest-value immediate empirical test is to link **same matched animal dates** to independently sourced environmental conditions across the already qualified cohorts, then test *within-population* climate associations and replicate taxa across contrasting habitat contexts. A prediction-score benchmark is optional; transparent ecological effect sizes, absolute day/night components, nulls and unqualified sources are essential. The work should not be submitted with a “universal seasonal hysteresis law” claim.
