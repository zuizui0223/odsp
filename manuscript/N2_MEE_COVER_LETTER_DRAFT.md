Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Beyond flat niche maps: separating added-axis thickness from transferable ecological organization,”** for publication in *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*.

Ecological niches are often represented in lower-dimensional maps even when organismal support varies along height, depth, time, behaviour or other state axes. Our manuscript introduces an information-theoretic evidence architecture that separates four inferential questions that are often conflated: whether an added ecological axis is estimable, how much state information remains after projection, whether that state is organized, and whether the organization transfers to independent observations.

The methodological contribution is not a new entropy formula in isolation. Instead, we connect conditional added-axis information, fitted organization and an explicit conditional-versus-marginal held-out log-score comparison within one fail-closed decision framework. The same generic API accepts arbitrary caller-declared base and added axes. Its finite-discrete-support genericity is validated by 1,873 deterministic proof obligations across randomized two- to six-dimensional support tensors, all of which passed with zero failures and a maximum absolute numerical error of 2.49 × 10⁻14.

We then demonstrate empirical portability across three substantially different public-data architectures. A Tawaki GPS-dive application terminates prospectively as structurally unavailable before biological thickness is opened. European free-tailed bat tracking retains substantial vertical thickness after horizontal location is known, yet conditioned vertical organization fails to improve prediction for either sealed individual. In contrast, Snapshot Serengeti camera detections are temporally broad, show species-time partitioning within sites, and yield positive independently held-out gains in all three cross-fitted site folds. Together, these applications demonstrate that added-axis thickness and transferable organization are empirically separable properties rather than a single binary notion of multidimensionality.

We believe the paper fits *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* because the central contribution is a broadly applicable inferential method, validated first with analytic and randomized benchmarks and then with empirical examples spanning different taxa, observation technologies, added-axis meanings and independent replication units. The manuscript also makes explicit what the framework does not establish: it does not infer universal biological outcomes, causal displacement, or automatic downstream state maps from terminal summary statistics.

For double-anonymous peer review, we provide a deterministic anonymized code-and-evidence archive. The archive includes the axis-agnostic method core, generality benchmarks, selected reproducibility tests, public-source empirical runners, the integrated anonymous manuscript, and sanitized terminal scientific summaries. It is built under an MIT open-source license, scanned for identifying tokens, and has been independently extracted, installed and tested in continuous integration.

The present study is a reanalysis of publicly archived datasets and involved no new animal capture, handling or field intervention. Ethical approvals and permits for the original data collection remain those reported by the corresponding source studies. Generative-AI assistance is disclosed transparently in the manuscript and title page; all scientific decisions, validation and final responsibility remain with the authors.
