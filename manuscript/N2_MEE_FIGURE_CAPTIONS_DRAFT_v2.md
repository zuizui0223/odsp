## Figure 1. Projection-aware inferential hierarchy for multidimensional ecological support

A non-negative ecological support distribution `S(B,A)` is indexed by retained/base axes `B` and added ecological axes `A`. Projection to a lower-dimensional representation marginalizes over `A`. The framework separates four inferential layers rather than treating multidimensionality as one binary property. **(1) Estimability** asks whether the added axis can be represented under a prospectively declared observation architecture. **(2) Thickness** quantifies state information remaining after the base state is known using `H(A|B)` and the effective-state equivalent `exp(H(A|B))`. **(3) Fitted organization** quantifies association among declared axes, for example `I(A;B)` or identity-time partitioning `I(C;T|B)`. **(4) Transferability** tests whether the conditioned distribution improves prediction in prospectively independent support relative to the lower-information marginal, `E[log P_model(A|B)-log P_model(A)]`. Independent groups are scored separately; a terminal generalizing result requires all predeclared group gains to be positive at the frozen zero-gain boundary. A positive result at one layer does not automatically satisfy the next. These quantities describe support under declared observation semantics and do not by themselves identify causal interactions or fundamental-niche volume.

## Figure 2. Known-truth and high-dimensional property tests validate the inferential core

**(A) Known-truth states.** Three analytically known support families demonstrate that fitted multidimensional organization and independent transfer are distinct. Thick but unorganized support contains four effective added states while `I(A;B)=0` and held-out gain `G=0`. Stable organization gives fitted information and held-out gain of `0.13081203594113697` nats. Shifted organization retains the same fitted `I(A;B)=0.13081203594113697` nats but reverses the organization in held-out support, yielding `G=-0.41849410839291784` nats. **(B) Axis-agnostic generality validation.** A deterministic benchmark evaluated 1,873 proof obligations over 128 random support tensors spanning two to six dimensions, 32 additional conditionally independent multi-added-axis systems, and one-, three- and seven-group transfer designs. All 1,873 obligations passed with zero failures and maximum absolute numerical error `2.4868995751603507e-14`. Validated properties included positive mass-scaling invariance, tensor-axis permutation equivariance, category-label invariance, omitted nuisance/refinement-axis invariance, the exact same-distribution identity `E[log P(A|B)-log P(A)]=I(A;B)`, multi-axis conditional-information composition, effective-state cardinality bounds and independent-group mass invariance. These tests establish representation-level genericity over finite discrete support tensors, not universal biological outcomes or freedom from observation bias.

## Figure 3. Three empirical lanes occupy distinct terminal states of the inferential hierarchy

Prospectively bounded empirical applications illustrate unavailable, thick/non-generalizing and thick/generalizing states. **(A) Tawaki:** the frozen GPS-dive architecture failed the full site-by-year structural gate before biological thickness was opened and terminated as `empirical_gate_d_unavailable`. This is an estimability result, not evidence for absent vertical structure. **(B) European free-tailed bat:** model-pool support retained `H(Z|X,Y)=1.3918623004770097` nats, equivalent to `4.022333876564191` effective fixed vertical states, but sealed-individual conditional-versus-marginal gains were `-0.43541033813280833` and `-0.021938657402345435`; the terminal state was thick but non-generalizing. **(C) Snapshot Serengeti:** 17 admitted mammal species yielded `H(T|Site)=1.6396235816361795` nats (`5.153229376935854` effective four-hour states), species-time partitioning `I(Species;T|Site)=0.22427598739601606` nats with permutation `p=0.005`, and positive cross-fitted site-fold gains of `0.0572411993741857`, `0.045158861333215006` and `0.04514355468571751`; the terminal category was `temporal_partition_generalizing`. Cross-system numerical values are not controlled effect-size comparisons: the systems differ in taxa, observation processes, conditioning variables and added-axis semantics. The contrast demonstrates heterogeneous portability of the inferential architecture and the existence of distinct terminal states rather than explaining why the systems differ.
