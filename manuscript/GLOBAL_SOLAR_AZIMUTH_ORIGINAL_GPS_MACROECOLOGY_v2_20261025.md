# Global variation in animal movement directions relative to the Sun: a source-wide original GPS macroecology

**ODSP manuscript v2 | 2026-10-25 | DRAFT / exploratory original-data macroecology and hourly wind/irradiance environmental linkage**

**Evidence declaration.** This study computes NEW directional ecological indices from publicly archived, original animal GPS records. It is NOT a meta-analysis of reported findings from original publications. Source authors and file licenses remain credited by their original DOIs. It is not a causal field experiment and has not established a global biological response to solar azimuth.

## Abstract

Solar azimuth defines a global moving compass reference, yet animal travel headings can covary with Sun position because of diel schedules, migration corridors and landscape orientation rather than direct use of sunlight. We established a provenance-preserving observational database by enumerating all **437** public Movebank Data Repository packages in a fixed 2026-10-19 snapshot, including **259** packages carrying GPS/GNSS sensor metadata. A non-outcome original-file inventory resolved all 259 bundles; bounded original CSV-header examination identified **159** packages with the four necessary timestamp, animal ID and latitude/longitude columns. From progressively frozen, source-selected original files, **40** archives passed their provider checksum and contained **14,756,835** GPS observation rows. Of these, **29** archives yielded at least one source×taxon cohort meeting predefined directional-movement criteria, giving **41 qualified cohorts across 32 taxon-name labels**, **1,295,932** eligible daylight movement intervals, and **3,351** source-group-summed animal qualifications, which are not independent worldwide population sample sizes. Raw sun-relative travel cosines were positive in 16 and negative in 25 cohorts. We decomposed raw alignment into an exact geographic-compass×solar marginal term and a joint direction–Sun circular covariance, finding that the two terms had larger magnitudes in 20 and 21 cohorts, respectively. Mule deer showed a raw positive mean in Utah (+0.02339) but a negative mean in Wyoming (−0.04876), with different relative contributions from the two terms. Across all nine named taxa observed in two source archives each, six retained the sign of the raw mean and three reversed, while five reversed the sign of the paired direction–Sun circular covariance; this post-result two-archive comparison is descriptive, not a phylogenetic or independent-population test. To test common ecological sampling support, we matched taxa within original source × 1° grid × year-month × four-hour apparent solar-clock cells. All ten preselected multi-taxon original sources yielded 17 species-pair contrasts; after a separately frozen serial technical recovery of five initially unavailable source downloads, four pairs from two studies met the shared-cell criterion. Three waterfowl pairs had directional differences +0.03944 (21 cells), −0.11814 (20) and +0.02227 (36); a tropical vulture pair differed by +0.03366 across 45 cells. These are observational, conditional species mean differences without causal solar-cue, competition or independent-biome inference. In a further original GPS × hourly ERA5 environmental analysis of the four previously supported pairs from two original study archives, all **40,060 original unique shared-frame daytime GPS intervals** linked to contemporaneous 100m wind/shortwave/temperature source model values, and all earlier solar-only pair contrasts replayed exactly. For the *Anser albifrons–Branta leucopsis* pair, the solar-relative travel difference was **−0.11814** while the same-source wind-flow-relative difference was **+0.01342**; their sampled wind-vs-solar geometry differed **−0.11829**. The weather relation is descriptive and not a causal decomposition. Our findings show empirically measurable, heterogeneous **observed sun-relative travel geometry** and nonuniform sampled meteorological opportunity, but climatic, geographic, sensor and movement-strategy causes remain unresolved. Original-data source coverage and source-independent population attribution are prerequisites for a causal or generalized macroecological account of solar-directional niches.

## Introduction

### The ecological question, not a coordinate-conversion exercise

At any geographical location and instant, sunlight has a particular azimuth and elevation. Moving animals experience this directional field alongside terrain, wind, vegetation, habitat, their own orientation and migratory routing. A single universal prediction such as "animals move toward sunlight" is not required. A more discriminating macroecological question is whether the *relation* between travel directions and astronomical solar bearings is organized systematically by movement ecology, seasonal environment and habitat geometry, and whether repeatable patterns survive conditioning on mundane clock and compass composition.

Originally archived GPS observations permit the calculation of a single transparent comparison quantity for multiple species, field populations and biomes; published paper effect sizes cannot supply the same observation denominator or resolution. The scientific innovation sought here is **cross-environment organization of solar-relative movement niches**, not new sunrise formulas, prediction-model score improvement or a reference-paper synthesis.

### Three distinct levels of evidence

**Level I—observational geometry.** Is there repeatable raw solar-relative travel alignment within original GPS source populations?

**Level II—context dependence.** Does the same species or the same source's taxon pair differ in the components of this alignment when observation scale and common ecological opportunities are matched?

**Level III—mechanism and generalization.** Are independent field-population differences associated with landscape aspect, habitat shade, prevailing wind relative to solar azimuth, thermal/water histories, phenology and movement guild, after accounting for source cadence, solar-angle availability and geographical dependence? Level III requires independent environmental linkage and is NOT established by the current results.

## Materials and methods

### Registry and study inclusion

The primary registry is the complete archival catalog frozen 2026-10-19. It has 437 public data packages; 259 metadata-label GPS/GNSS. Public source packages are NOT statistically independent biomes, populations or even necessarily disjoint animal deployments. Three study-ID values occur on more than one GPS/GNSS package (six packages total); two GPS/GNSS packages lack study IDs. Every archive and species label must retain its DOI, study identity, original license, original checksum and all recorded source holds.

First, all 259 original source bitstream lists were resolved; 670 data-container filename-suffix hints arose, but a suffix is not a verified original GPS schema. Bounded 8-KB original header-only screening found 159 canonical 4-column plain source CSVs; the remaining 100 were classified as multi-file/ambiguous (62), over original transfer cap (29), compressed (5) or missing required columns (4). No animal observations were read by that screen.

Next, a blinded registry metadata sample of 12 new sources was selected from 186 metadata-qualified candidates, in four combinations of species-label count × reported animal count, with three fixed SHA-hash ranks per combination and no source substitution on errors. It contributed seven checksum-verified usable source files, eight qualified cohort results, plus all source and animal-support holds. Subsequent header-supported sample was fixed at 24 further primary CSV original UUIDs (all ten remaining multi-taxon sources plus fourteen single-taxon SHA-ranked sources) before new GPS direction values were exposed. All 24 source original files passed integrity and original event analysis on the frozen first run; 20 source×taxon groups met the n>=5 independent tagged-animal descriptive threshold. Historic original nine-source pilots had already been analyzed for different temporal estimands and are labeled post-prior-outcome exploratory, never newly untouched validation.

### GPS and astronomy

Let B be the initial great-circle compass bearing, clockwise from geographic north, of an original same-tag 0.5–2h GPS displacement; let A be the approximate NOAA geometric solar azimuth at the original interval midpoint and its real location. Daylight moving intervals require original displaced distance >=100m, speed <=60km/h and solar elevation >=10° at original start, midpoint and end. No original sun-day crossing is allowed. Time-only nearest-UTC-hour-start selection was applied for certain existing sources and all new v1/v2 sources; historic source-specific cadence selection may differ, precluding naïve global effect pooling.

Define raw sunward *travel* mean R = E[cos(B−A)]. An axial alternative E[cos(2(B−A))] is stored; neither describes animal head/body orientation or sunlight exposure. The **exact circular identity** within an individual sample is

R = (E[cos B] E[cos A] + E[sin B] E[sin A])
  + (Cov(cos B, cos A) + Cov(sin B, sin A)),

which separates an expected projection from the independent observed geographic-compass/Sun marginals (G) and additional paired circular covariance (J). It is a statistical identity, not a causal mediation result. We compare source groups with animal-equal and year-equal weight.

A distinct conditional response estimates circular covariance only from within-animal, within-calendar-month, two-hour apparent-solar-clock strata with >=8 source GPS intervals, >=3 solar dates and >=5° original azimuth span. An eligible animal-year requires >=30 intervals over >=5 days, a source taxon generally >=5 eligible animals. The conditional response and the raw R use **different observational support**, therefore their ratio is NOT an attenuation estimate. A null/unsupported conditional cell is not absence of solar sensory behavior.

### Across-taxon ecological common support

After inspecting marginal source results, we separately froze a **post-exposure exploratory** common-support route for all ten v2 multi-taxon primary original sources. Species pairs compare solar-relative movement only within identical source × 1° latitude-longitude grid × calendar year-month × 4-hour apparent-solar-clock stratum. For each taxon and stratum require >=3 tagged animals, >=3 original solar dates and >=15 qualifying steps. Species pairs require >=3 common strata; mean per-species animal-equal solar alignment is compared equally over shared strata. All combinations failing common support remain undefined; grid locations, exact original dates and animal IDs stay inside the runner. This does not demonstrate fine-scale encounters, interactions or competition. Descriptive original-source animal bootstrap percentile intervals are not familywise or independent-region significance tests.

### Hourly meteorological context: wind direction and shortwave radiation

After the first solar-azimuth marginal and same-source results were available, we preregistered a **post-result exploratory** independent ERA5 environmental linkage for both source archives in which at least one taxon pair qualified for the original shared 1° grid × year-month × 4-hour apparent solar clock comparisons. Source UUIDs, four original native species pairs, unchanged GPS observational gates and original species-pair solar-only effects were frozen before first weather values. Original GPS files were retrieved and provider-MD5 verified again; exact original source pair contrasts and eligible source cells were reproduced **before any climate API call**.

At each original GPS moving step midpoint in a previously admitted shared cell, hourly ERA5 (explicit model = era5, never blended default) supplied 100m wind speed (m/s), 100m meteorological wind direction (FROM, clockwise from north), surface shortwave radiation (W/m², preceding-hour average), and modeled 2m temperature (°C) through Open-Meteo's historical public archive. UTC is used throughout with exact original source-grid-year requests at original coarse 1° grid centers. Wind flow direction W was defined as FROM + 180°, modulo 360°, and we computed GPS travel relative to wind **cos(B−W)** and meteorological wind-to-solar geometrical alignment **cos(W−A)** using the *same source GPS steps* as the solar relative **cos(B−A)**. GPS step timing, source identity and individual IDs stay in the ephemeral analysis; only anonymous source×taxon contrasts, weather provenance SHA256s and source counts are archived.

Within each original shared stratum, mean every variable first within each original animal, then average the admitted animals within taxon and weigh common source cells equally. A source pair is environmentally described only if at least 90% of prequalified original source steps are matched and all original common cells have modeled hourly weather. Source response SHA-256 and original GPS source MD5 are retained; raw ERA5 weather JSON bodies and animal locations are not committed. New climatological results are observational, do not measure true flying-altitude wind or bodily solar irradiation, and lack independent-field or source-animal inferential uncertainty.

## Results

### Original-data universe and quantitative support

Provider checksums and original source analyses authenticated **40** original source files. Of those, **29** each supply at least one qualified taxon group, for **41** source×taxon effect rows over **32** literal scientific names. These represent **14,756,835** original GPS rows, with **1,295,932** daytime directional intervals in n>=5 admitted groups and summed **3,351** qualified source taxon animal records. Additional unsupported animal observations/source taxa and source technical holds remain in the [complete derived matrix](../research/GLOBAL_SOLAR_AZIMUTH_COMBINED_V2_ALL_ORIGINAL_SOURCE_TAXON_MATRIX_20261025.csv) and [full summary](../research/GLOBAL_SOLAR_AZIMUTH_COMBINED_V2_MACRO_SUMMARY_20261025.json). These sample totals are NOT n=41 independent ecosystems, nor 3,351 verified distinct biological individuals worldwide.

Among 41 source×taxon means, R was positive for **16** and negative for **25**; abs(G)>abs(J) in **20** and abs(J)>=abs(G) in **21**. Source-conditional exploratory animal-bootstrap percentile intervals exclude zero in 16/41 raw source groups and 5/41 conditional source groups. No multiple testing correction, phylogenetic regression, independent-population interval, causal environmental adjustment or probability of future study generalization has yet been established.

### Within-species variation and decomposition

A contrast in two independent-source Utah and Wyoming mule deer original archives illustrates that the signed raw pattern is not a fixed species property: Utah R = **+0.023393** (1,652 eligible animals), decomposed G = +0.003049 and J = +0.020344; Wyoming R = **−0.048758** (110 animals), G = −0.042838 and J = −0.005920. In the distinct conditional solar-time-stratified estimand, Utah +0.000305 (animal-bootstrap interval −0.000071..+0.000623, n1509) and Wyoming −0.000743 (−0.002772..+0.001236, n106) each included zero. These data show *different observational decomposition*, not ecological seasonality or alternative sun compass mechanisms.

African elephant source cohorts similarly differ in raw signed means, +0.03314 (Etosha) versus −0.00903 (Kruger), but the Kruger bootstrap interval spans zero and the individual samples are small (15 vs14). Among two red fox sources, R was negative in both (−0.0430 vs −0.1053), and two barnacle goose sources were positive (+0.01355 vs +0.08280); raw magnitudes also include original study-year/device, sample-region and movement-route differences. The latter are examples of the current source matrix, not selected independent replication proof. A post-result comprehensive audit of the nine taxon labels each qualifying in exactly two original source archives finds **6/9** signed raw means concordant and **3/9** reversed; **4/9** signed paired direction–solar circular covariance terms concordant and **5/9** reversed. The relative larger-magnitude marginal versus joint term changes category between paired original sources for **four** of these nine taxon labels. This is a descriptive sample-specific sensitivity of temporal-directional architecture, **not** a replicated population law or significance test ([complete two-source audit](../research/GLOBAL_SOLAR_AZIMUTH_WITHIN_TAXON_TWO_SOURCE_REPEATABILITY_AUDIT_20261025.json)).

### Across-species within-common-stratum contrast

Among the ten fixed v2 multi-taxon original archives, exactly **17** original species-pair combinations were considered. The first shared-cell workflow authenticated five original rereads, with five others SOURCE_HOLD (three MD5 discrepancies, two remote disconnects). A separately frozen, one-off *serial* rerun reproduced original provider MD5 identity for all five previously held sources under the **unchanged** geometry and source-cell rules; first-run statuses remain preserved. The combined result now supports **four** same-source, matched-grid/month/4h-clock comparisons in **two** original ecological study archives. Within one waterfowl archive, *Anser albifrons* minus *Anser anser* is **+0.039442** across 21 shared strata (26 vs 10 tagged animals; one coarse spatial grid); *Anser albifrons* minus *Branta leucopsis* is **−0.118143** across 20 strata (14 vs 53 animals; three coarse grids); *Anser anser* minus *Branta leucopsis* is **+0.022268** across 36 strata (12 vs 39 animals; two coarse grids). The second source has two tropical vulture taxa *Cathartes melambrotus* and *Sarcoramphus papa* with equal-shared-cell R_A − R_B = **+0.033663** across 45 shared strata in two coarse grids and 15 year-months, five original individuals per species. No valid independent-source p-value, interval or causal result follows. The *Vulpes lagopus* and *Vulpes vulpes* source still has **zero** supported matched strata despite opposite unconditioned raw signs; do not claim co-occurring fox directional-niche partitioning.

The five historical first-run source holds remain unchanged in [the first source receipt](../research/GLOBAL_SOLAR_AZIMUTH_MULTI_SOURCE_SHARED_CELL_V0_FIRST_RESULT_20261025.json). Their MD5-verified serial reread is archived separately as [technical recovery v1](../research/GLOBAL_SOLAR_AZIMUTH_SHARED_CELL_SOURCE_SERIAL_REPLAY_V1_FIRST_RESULT_20261025.json), and the ten-source, 17-pair combined observational result is [here](../research/GLOBAL_SOLAR_AZIMUTH_MULTITAXON_SHARED_CELL_COMBINED_FROZEN_V0_V1_20261025.json). This recovery is not a second biological replicate.

### Original shared-species directional organization versus wind and radiation

Both original source archives passed source-provider integrity and the previously frozen original solar-only pair numerical replay **before** evaluating new weather: three waterfowl pairs and one tropical vulture pair. In **all four pairs**, **100%** of the original eligible GPS steps had matching modeled ERA5 weather. There were **13 original ERA5 source grid×UTC calendar-year requests** and **40,060 original GPS steps in the union of paired shared-cell frames** (25,567 goose-source and 14,493 vulture-source; overlapping pairs reuse goose-source steps). These observations add two positive, narrowly framed ecological insights. First, for *A. albifrons* against *B. leucopsis*, the signed difference in solar-relative original travel is negative while the same observed movement steps' difference in downwind-relative travel is weakly positive. The meteorological *wind-vs-sun* directional geometry sampled by these two taxa differs by −0.11829, almost the same magnitude as their signed solar movement difference. This is **not a numerical decomposition of solar effects into weather causes**: the weather-only opportunity distribution differs because the observed source GPS times and locations differ even after broad space/month/solar-clock matching. Second, the vulture source's same-support taxon difference in original mean shortwave is −36.91 W/m², demonstrating unequal source gridded weather exposure across taxa within equal coarse astronomical strata, not a directly observed preference for shade or solar irradiation.

The four species pairs involve only **two field archives**, not four independent landscapes. We have neither source-animal error intervals for these new environment differences nor experimental interventions in sunshine or wind. The wind field is ERA5 100m, whereas actual birds fly at other heights, and cloud cover and radiative exposure are not independently measured at the animal's body. The exact trigonometric identity cos(B−A) = cos(B−W)cos(W−A) − sin(B−W)sin(W−A) does not permit a causal "percent explained by wind" inference from separately averaged cosine moments, because products/covariances and potential habitat/time confounding remain.

## Discussion

**Conservative positive ecological conclusion.** Animals across the registered source population sample show substantial *descriptive variation in the geometry of original daytime travel headings relative to astronomical solar azimuth*. This variation is neither invariably sunward nor antisunward and the relative marginal-compass and paired circular-covariance components can differ across independent original source recruitment contexts, including within a named species. Within-source shared-opportunity comparisons are now estimable for four species pairs from two original studies; 13 of 17 tested pairs remain unsupported under the fixed spatial/season/solar-clock criteria. None of these four observational contrasts has an independent population uncertainty analysis.

**Important negative identification finding.** A raw sun-relative cosine is not an animal sun-orientation mechanism, and common timestamps and angles alone cannot separate actual solar cue use from routes, migration corridors, terrain, diurnal seasonality, source GPS cadence and environmental history. Diel/clock-conditioned covariance uses reduced within-time azimuth contrasts and need not be the same biological estimand. The shared pair does not establish trophic avoidance, predator competition or partitioning of an observable sun-derived niche.

**Environmental comparison now performed, but mechanism remains open.** The hourly same-source original GPS × ERA5 results show weather opportunity is not constant within the original coarse matched solar strata; solar-relative and wind-relative taxon differences are not universally identical. In particular, the three goose pairs exhibit different signed combinations across solar, downwind and wind-sun geometry, so a single simplistic wind-following explanation is not established. More independent field populations, local terrain-aspect/canopy/wind and exact original tagged-animal deployment support must be investigated before attempting a general ecological thermal or directional niche law. The weather first receipt carries provider raw response SHA256s only; previous ODSP work found weather refetch variability, so future numerical replay may require a version-matched frozen response body.

**Next macroecological test.** To move from the present multi-source descriptive database to broad ecological inference, separately enumerate independent geographically disjoint source populations and record measured environmental covariates at original sampling locations: surface solar flux and terrain-aspect interaction with Sun altitude/azimuth, wind vector relative to migration bearing, habitat canopy or vegetation seasonal phase, ERA5 temperature/water history, and behavioral movement guild. Then estimate whether source-population directional geometry co-varies with those environmental contexts, with nested individual/year/site/source terms and published original sample selection; retain all no-support and negative data. No new positive worldwide interaction effect is asserted yet.
