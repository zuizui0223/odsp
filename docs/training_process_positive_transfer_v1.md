# Training-process directional transfer inference v1

## Why this is a separate route

The existing all-refit route asks a literal fixed-set question:

> Did every supplied upstream refit support the required positive transfer step?

That is an intersection-union claim about a particular supplied set. It does not
treat those refits as a probability sample and it does not generalize to an
unsampled training process.

This route asks a different question:

> If the upstream model is generated by one predeclared training-resampling
> process, is the mean held-out directional gain from that process positive on
> the validation population?

The two claims are intentionally non-equivalent. A process-mean claim can pass
even when one observed refit is negative. That does not rescue the fixed-set
claim; it means the inferential target is different.

## Estimand

For validation group g and information step c, let D(r, v; g, c) be the held-out
gain produced by training-process draw r and validation draw v. The v1 target is
the crossed expectation

E_r E_v D(r, v; g, c).

It is not the minimum over refits, the probability that a future individual
refit passes, or a statement that all possible refits are positive.

## Crossed bootstrap

With R observed refits from the frozen process, every bootstrap replicate:

1. resamples R refit identities with replacement once;
2. independently resamples validation blocks once within each validation group;
3. applies the same refit resample to every group and information step;
4. applies the same group-specific block resample to every selected refit and
   every contrast in that group.

The validation blocks are not redrawn separately for each refit. Doing so would
incorrectly make one validation dataset look like R independent validation
datasets.

The v1 point studentizer combines two first-order components: the standard error
of refit-specific group gains across training-process draws and the cluster-ratio
standard error across validation blocks after averaging the refits. The crossed
interaction remains present in the bootstrap distribution, but v1 does not
separately identify an interaction variance component in the studentizer.

That last point is exactly why this module is not yet a confirmatory route.
Prospective null calibration must show that the resulting one-sided max-t
procedure actually controls the advertised familywise error before routing can
be promoted.

## Required provenance

A numeric refit-by-validation score tensor cannot establish that its rows are
valid draws from the intended process. Confirmatory use therefore requires a
pre-outcome process manifest that freezes the resampling population and units,
stratification or blocking rules, resampling algorithm, number of refits, RNG
and fit-seed policy, fitting and preprocessing implementation, and the
training-validation separation rules. The generated refits then need a receipt
that binds them back to that manifest.

The current numeric core accepts a process ID and manifest SHA256 so that this
future provenance layer has a hard attachment point. It does not manufacture
that provenance itself.

## Relationship to existing ODSP routes

Nothing here reclassifies existing evidence.

- Fixed-set all-refit intersection remains the method for the claim that every
  supplied refit passes.
- The historical nested refit-mixture machinery remains sensitivity-only.
- Existing frozen empirical results remain frozen under their original route.
- The new process route remains experimental until its own prospective
  operating-characteristic and governance evidence is frozen.
- Untouched external validation needs a separate process-specific freeze
  endpoint after the internal route has been qualified.

The intended eventual chain is therefore:

validation-sample uncertainty -> upstream training-process uncertainty ->
untouched external validation,

with each arrow qualified on its own inferential target rather than borrowing
evidence from the fixed-set route.
