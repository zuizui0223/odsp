Date: 2026-10-19  
Status: central hypothesis, registry source inventories and first exploratory independent 21-site ERA5 environment grid; animal-level database assembly and comparative animal response tests remain incomplete.

## 研究の中心

**太陽の暦と、その動物が経験する環境の暦のずれは、動物の昼夜の移動配分をどう変えるか。**

同じ個体を、日長がほぼ等しい年内の二時点で比べる。それでも温度や植生の生育段階が違えば、移動する時間帯も変わるという仮説を立てる。目標は、季節差の大きさと方向、その環境条件との関連、種や個体群による共通性と違いを明らかにすることである。別の地域を高精度に予測することは必須条件にしない。場所を変えて環境を追える個体が、時間帯の変更を少なくできるかは、関連する第二の問いとする。

自前で構築するものは、公開された原観測に、出典・個体・観測機会・太陽位相・環境の季節進行を結び付けた比較データベースである。原観測の採集者の著者性とライセンスを維持し、ODSPの貢献を独自の仮説、系統的な統合、比較単位、解析、再利用可能な派生データとして明示する。

## 1. Central hypothesis

**At matched photoperiod within an individual, seasonal differences in diel movement allocation are associated with thermal conditions and vegetation phenophase; the magnitude and direction of those relationships vary with feeding ecology and movement strategy.**

This is a hypothesis about ecological organization of a temporal niche. The response is when an animal moves; the proposed explanatory state is the seasonal environment where it lives. It does not assume all species become more nocturnal in one calendar season. A wet-season landscape and a cold-season landscape can show different directions for the same increasing/decreasing-daylength label.

The scientific targets are the magnitude and direction of seasonal movement differences, their association with environmental contrasts, and the ecological conditions under which populations share or differ in those relationships. Multiple independently sampled populations support comparative inference about commonality and heterogeneity. They need not be divided into training and prediction sets for the primary analysis. Forecasting an unobserved population is a possible later application, not the definition of a successful ecological study.

### Why the existing positive results motivate this question

The [Serengeti satellite linkage](serengeti_original_camera_modis_ndvi_photo_link_20261015.md) found greater declining-branch NDVI in all 322 supported camera-site × annual-cycle comparisons at near-equal daylength. Annual mean differences were +0.1646, +0.2727 and +0.2224. Thus the astronomical match did not make the vegetation states equivalent in that landscape. The [camera decomposition](serengeti_within_photo_time_versus_station_mix_ecology_20261015.md) also found repeated within-camera changes in zebra photo timing, although changing station contributions could reinforce or oppose those changes in other taxa.

These results generate the question; they do not establish the mechanism. The [NDVI association analysis](serengeti_ndvi_level_vs_rank_photo_ecology_20261016.md) did not find a consistent vegetation–photo response across the few supported taxon-years. The [duck GPS audit](duck_gps_identifiability_and_next_ecology_20261019.md) found zero median instantaneous speed in 709 of 744 day/night blocks. That endpoint is not a sound basis for a general movement-allocation result. The subsequent [two-part sensor description](duck_gps_reported_speed_occurrence_and_magnitude_20261019.md) separates relative night/day changes from absolute changes within each time window, but adds no independent animals or validated activity measurement. Serengeti, Rhode Island, and the previously inspected duck archive are discovery data, not untouched confirmation data.

The next empirical object is therefore a database of repeated individuals and their environments, assembled across source registries. Camera information supplies a separate observation channel where deployment support permits a comparable seasonal question.

### New independent environmental context: 21-site, three-cycle equinox grid (2026-10-19)

A separate exploratory **environmental** sampling frame was locally fixed before the new ERA5 outcomes, rather than selecting field studies or sites with favorable animal signals: 72 original latitude–longitude points, a low-resolution static land mask retaining **21** land points, and three equinox-rising versus equinox-falling source-date pairs per location in 2018–2020. The original paired astronomical daylength mismatch never exceeded **0.7998 minute**. Original 12 batch ERA5 temperature and surface 0–7cm soil-moisture API responses are now saved in the [source-replayed global-grid report](global_equinoctial_ERA5_21land_sites_2018_2020_ecological_results.md); the [replay workflow 39201993065](https://github.com/zuizui0223/odsp/actions/runs/39201993065) passed all six tests and archived every fixed pair, including the one physically invalid soil-moisture datum **−0.001 m³/m³** (Iran 2019) as HOLD. Thus **62/63** original pair-years admit both variables, and **29/62** show **opposing signs of air-temperature and soil-water change** at near-equal daylength (18 warmer/drier and 11 cooler/wetter). Five fixed sites show opposite signs in all three original annual pairs, in either direction. At a fixed Argentine site, averaging three opposed annual thermal–hydrologic vectors conceals that every individual year had the opposite-sign relationship.

This is a quantitative, geographically broader source observation that **solar-daylength matching does not fix one common environmental seasonal state**. It DOES NOT establish any animal timing or movement response across these regions, and the 21 sites are neither area-weighted nor representative of global land area or independent ecosystems. Shallow modeled water is not edible forage, and single-day environmental comparisons can reflect weather. The new positive environmental result motivates the **vector** of environment contrasts (e.g. local ΔT, Δwater and future measured greenness), not an assumption that decreasing daylight universally means hotter/drier or wetter. Within-population animal time-use associations remain to be tested from the separately registered public animal source cohort; an externally held-out prediction model remains an optional later analysis, **not a prerequisite** for a defensible comparative ecological finding.

### Time-scale check of the ecological seasonal state (same 21 sites; 2026-10-19)

The [15/30-day ERA5 source-replayed history sensitivity](global_fixed_21sites_ERA5_multiday_environment_history_20261019.md) now uses the **same 63 fixed site×year rising/falling photoperiod endpoints**, with no new geography or astronomy date selection, and separate last-15-day and last-30-day local climate histories. Original API daily values and the original single-day source endpoint values were verified; [workflow 39210495523](https://github.com/zuizui0223/odsp/actions/runs/39210495523) passed **7 tests**. Source-valid site-year contrasts with opposite directions of changing 2m mean T and shallow-soil moisture remain substantial at **26/59** (15d) and **26/57** (30d), compared with **29/62** (original one day).

The key complication is **time-scale-dependent membership**: among the 57 original site×cycle observations source-valid at all three history windows, **26/57** were opposed at one day and also **26/57** at thirty days, but **eight originally opposed pairs lost the classification while eight different pairs gained it**; only **16/57** remained opposed at 1, 15 and 30 days. Thus a stable *count* across environmental histories can hide changing populations and regional seasonal paths. Different timescales have unequal missingness because physically impossible negative ERA5 soil water occurred on some source days (no imputation).

Biological consequence remains **hypothetical**: heat exposure and resource/water conditions should be assigned to an organism's response using explicitly defined antecedent windows, and ecological-history sensitivity should be distinguished from revising the response model after seeing animals. In this pilot there are **no animal observations at the 21 source locations**, and center photoperiod is matched only at the history endpoint, not across every day in its trailing window. The present data do not identify the animal-relevant integration horizon, a causal thermal effect or a universal population pattern. Multiple independent tagged populations from the already frozen public-data registry remain necessary for the comparative ecological question.

## 2. Ecological questions, expected patterns, and competing explanations

| Ecological question / expectation | Main comparison | Interpretation of contrary or weak evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Q1: seasonal asymmetry.** How large and widespread are diel movement differences between opposite seasons at matched daylength? | Estimate paired within-individual night/day and daylight-core/edge contrasts, then their distribution among populations and taxa, accounting for observation support. | Precise near-zero contrasts bound asymmetry in that group; wide intervals indicate limited information. Neither result is hidden. An overall mean near zero can coexist with opposing group differences. |
| **H1: environmental association.** Seasonal movement contrasts covary with thermal and vegetation contrasts, with differences among feeding guilds and movement strategies. | Estimate prespecified within-individual environmental associations and trait interactions, keeping calendar branch and available biological-season variables. Separate within-individual changes from between-population differences. | Precise near-zero environmental terms do not support the proposed explanation in that domain. Residual seasonal asymmetry can remain even when the measured environmental explanation is unsupported. |
| **H2: conditional heat avoidance.** Greater daytime thermal burden is associated with less movement during hot daylight hours. | Estimate a directional thermal term with separate taxon/guild responses and measured risk modifiers. Report absolute daytime and nighttime rates as well as their relative contrast. | A precise opposite or near-zero association limits this mechanism in the specified stratum. Reduced daytime movement alone does not establish an absolute nighttime increase. |

H1 is the central explanatory hypothesis; Q1 establishes the pattern it seeks to explain. H2 is a directional mechanism-specific expectation, not an explanation already proved by the Serengeti pilot. These are expectations about observed ecological relationships, not requirements to win a prediction benchmark. Vegetation greenness does not imply a universal positive or negative effect on night use. The initial vegetation representation is level plus independently estimated phenophase/change, with prespecified guild interactions; interpretation concerns vegetation state, not directly measured forage quality. Diet and movement strategy must come from documented classifications or an independently defined movement measure; do not define a trait by the same seasonal response it is meant to explain.

An endogenous annual state, breeding, migration stage, moonlight, predation, and human use can also produce seasonal differences. Matching daylength does not remove these explanations. In particular, [Daan and Aschoff (1975)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00345851) already reported unequal spring/autumn activity duration at equal photoperiod in captive finches. We retain calendar branch in the baseline and report any residual branch effect after measured environmental predictors.

**Human-clock question.** Where a source contains measured visitor, vehicle, livestock, or human-detection timing, test whether wildlife timing follows that schedule after solar and environmental conditions are included. A civil time zone, daylight-saving label, road map, or human-footprint score alone is not a measurement of the human daily schedule. Static human-pressure variables may modify responses but cannot identify a clock-entrainment mechanism.

**Secondary spatial question.** Does changing location reduce the environmental contrast an individual experiences, and is this associated with less temporal redistribution? Compare used locations with an explicitly defined accessible or reference environment. This is initially an observational association: habitat is chosen by the animal. Keep moving individuals in the cohort. A same-cell-only restriction would remove much of the proposed response and cannot by itself test a spatial/temporal substitution mechanism.

## 3. Sampling frame: enumerate databases before choosing studies

The first inventory was retrieved on 2026-10-19. It is a dated discovery frame, not an estimate of the number of eligible animals or independent ecological studies.

| --- | --- | ---: | --- |

| GBIF dataset registry | All records returned by `endpointType=CAMTRAP_DP` | 26 dataset records, 26 distinct dataset keys | Complementary camera/deployment discovery frame |

The Movebank inventory includes 258 packages explicitly listing GPS and 47 listing acceleration; these labels can overlap. Publication dates are not tracking dates. There are 413 distinct nonblank source study-ID strings across the 419 packages reporting that field; a study ID still does not define an independent population. The GBIF registry contains heterogeneous monitoring designs; a Camtrap DP endpoint does not establish a suitable mammal camera series.

Every source remains in the registry through screening. Acquisition decisions use source permissions, modalities, temporal/geographical coverage, and the ecological scope defined here, not an article's reported sign or statistical significance. Existing abstracts and pilot results have been read, so this is outcome-independent selection by rule, not a claim that investigators have never seen published outcomes.

Begin with free-ranging terrestrial mammals for the primary biological comparison. Keep birds as a separately specified extension and do not pool flight movement, terrestrial displacement, accelerometer activity, and camera detections into a common numeric activity endpoint. Domestic, captive, marine, and incompatible-sensor records remain in the inventory with explicit scope labels. Resolve multi-taxon packages at the animal level. No source is removed merely because its effect is null, heterogeneous, or opposite to the hypothesis.

### What the own database will contain

| Table / unit | Required content | Scientific purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source package | Stable source DOI/key, version, license statements, file hashes, source study IDs, original attribution, screening state and reason | Reproducible sampling frame and provenance |

| Individual-day / solar interval | Solar geometry, actual available day/night hours, observed displacement or validated activity, temporal coverage | Estimate an interpretable timing response |

Deduplicate original event keys or study-qualified individual/time records after resolving versions and overlapping studies. Do not sum package-level animal/location counts and call them unique animals or fixes. Retain raw source values and an explicit mapping to normalized variables.

Public metadata do not settle raw-file reuse terms: preserve all license statements and resolve conflicts before redistribution. A raw-source license need not match the code license. When redistribution is unavailable, release the lawful derived tables plus source references and acquisition code. The inventory contains no newly published precise animal trajectories.

## 4. Analysis decisions before new biological effects are opened

### Outcome and observation unit

GPS measures displacement between positions. It is not total activity or energetic expenditure. Use ground displacement over elapsed time on a declared, source-compatible cadence, with day/night availability and coverage; acceleration may supply a separate validated activity outcome where calibration is documented. Do not reuse the old median-instantaneous-speed endpoint or choose a speed threshold because it yields a positive result.

For the first GPS analysis, target 60-minute displacement intervals from sources with reliable timestamps, positions, and a compatible schedule. Select fixes by time only; retain actual elapsed intervals and avoid filling long gaps with artificial straight-line hourly fixes. Estimate distance per observed hour separately for night, daylight core, and daylight edge. Day/night exposure differs by season, so raw fix counts or total distances alone are not allocation measures. A two-part observation model may handle zero and positive displacement, but its measurement-error rule must be fixed from sensor documentation or independent calibration, not the desired ecological contrast. Sources unable to support this cadence remain documented for other resolutions.

Basic repeated unit: individual × annual cycle. Independent ecological replication: biological population and recruitment study, then region. Account for archive overlap and shared recruitment in the grouping structure; use hierarchical estimation or uncertainty calculations at the actual independent sampling level. Resampling individual fixes would understate uncertainty. Holding out whole populations is optional for a later predictive question, not a required step in estimating ecological associations.

### Daylength and environmental state

Compute solar geometry from actual date, UTC time, and location. For mobile animals, distinguish astronomical daylength at the used location from the seasonal branch at a fixed study reference latitude. Latitude changes during movement can change experienced daylength; do not label that as a change in the Earth's seasonal branch. Define individual-cycle pairing using increasing/decreasing daylength at that fixed reference, match actual experienced daylength, and keep the distance/latitude difference as part of the ecological record. Analyze cross-hemisphere migrants separately if a single reference cycle is unsuitable.

Within a cycle, match opposite-branch dates using daylight and observation support without reading movement magnitude. Start with a five-minute daylength tolerance and at least 30 days between paired dates; use each day once with a maximum-cardinality, minimum-mismatch assignment. This prevents adjacent near-solstice dates from supplying a nominal opposite-season comparison. Report the supported daylength range; at near-equatorial sites daylength alone provides little separation, making the explicit date/phase separation especially important.

Screen timestamp/cadence/deployment coverage before examining effect signs. The initial support table reports qualifying pairs under 5/10/20 paired-day and 50/75/90% scheduled-fix coverage thresholds. These are design feasibility summaries, not nine alternative hypothesis tests. Select and commit one primary support rule using precision simulations and coverage only, before computing new animal movement effects. Record the final population roster, dependence groups, ecological contrasts, and moderator definitions at that same point. This document is the programme specification, not a claim that this later event-level freeze has already occurred.

Join a versioned temperature series and satellite vegetation product to used locations and dates. For the primary interval-level environmental association, locate explanatory variables at the interval's starting position or a pre-response reference area; do not use a location reached during the movement being explained. Reserve whole-track experienced conditions for descriptive exposure and the secondary spatial analysis. This maintains temporal ordering and limits circular interpretation of habitat selection, although it does not establish causation. Derive daylight-versus-night temperature, vegetation level and growth/senescence phase, with one predeclared contemporary representation and one fixed lag representation. Use environmental data to derive phenophase without fitting to animal response. NDVI is a vegetation index; no direct forage-quality claim follows. Regional air temperature is an exposure proxy; it is not measured body temperature or the animal's selected microclimate.

### Estimating patterns and environmental associations

First report paired within-individual seasonal contrasts and their uncertainty, with the independent population/taxon distribution. Then use the same outcome, observations, observation model, and temporal resolution to estimate three sets of ecological quantities:

1. **Seasonal pattern at matched solar conditions:** within-individual branch differences, with the remaining daylength mismatch and taxon/population structure accounted for.
2. **Environmental association:** how paired thermal and vegetation differences relate to the seasonal movement contrast, retaining annual phase/branch and independently supplied biological-season variables. Separate within-individual changes from between-individual or between-region environmental differences.
3. **Ecological heterogeneity:** how the associations differ with documented feeding guild, movement strategy, and region, using prespecified interactions and an appropriate population/species structure. Consider phylogenetic dependence for cross-species trait comparisons when the sampled scope supports it.

Assess evidence through effect sizes, uncertainty, variation among independent units, and the adequacy of the observation model. Display null and opposing associations alongside shared patterns. Avoid allowing one source's many fixes to stand in for many independently sampled populations. Quantify ecological commonality by the distribution of relationships and their dependence on conditions; it does not require every species to share a sign. State where the sampled public sources limit the target population. Failure to improve a held-out prediction score is not a rejection criterion for these ecological quantities.

Show estimated ecological contrasts in familiar units: daytime and nighttime displacement rates, their seasonal changes, relative night/day differences, and redistribution from daylight core to edge. A shift in relative allocation can arise from daytime decline, nighttime increase, or both, so show the absolute components. These effects and their environmental/trait associations are the scientific results the programme seeks. Model checking supports their interpretation; predictive benchmarking is optional.

Camera data use their own effort-aware detection endpoint and model. Agreement of independently measured channels can strengthen interpretation; their numerical values are not interchangeable. Lack of opposite-season support in a camera archive does not block the primary movement analysis.

## 5. Prior work and the intended contribution

Equal-photoperiod seasonal differences are not a new observation: [Daan & Aschoff 1975](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00345851) explicitly studied them. Solar transformation is also established ([Vazquez et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13290)). Broad comparative diel plasticity has a recent large-scale treatment ([Devarajan et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ado3843)). Resource phenology and spatial movement tactics already have cross-population tests ([Aikens et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.06.032)). Recent biologging studies also analyze temperature, vegetation, and daily or seasonal activity ([2025 antelope study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40462-025-00604-y); [2021 ibex study](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13750)).

The intended contribution combines an explicitly enumerated public-source cohort, repeated-individual opposite-season contrasts, environmental associations, and comparative estimates of taxon/population heterogeneity. The empirical question is how diel seasonal asymmetry relates to ecological conditions and which aspects are shared across species and regions. Predictive accuracy is not the novelty claim or the publication criterion. This is a proposed contribution to establish with data; this source review does not justify a claim of being the first study.
