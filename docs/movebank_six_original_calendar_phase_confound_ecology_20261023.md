# Does the apparent temperature–diel movement relation survive the seasonal calendar?

**2026-10-23 | ODSP PR #267 | REAL source-verified ecological falsifier — exploratory POST earlier outcomes, not a preregistered independent animal experiment.**

## Core ecological question

Earlier original public Movebank telemetry analyses showed differing NIGHT-relative diel movement contrasts among **six recruitment cohorts (197 individually tagged animals)** at approximately equal astronomical daylength, despite consistently warmer decreasing-daylight periods at one reference ERA5 grid point per recruitment region. When the original matched-date regional temperature differences were related to original GPS day/night displacement at the SAME individual×year, the Wyoming mule deer and Virginia white-tailed deer cohorts had positive mean thermal associations, while the Connecticut black bear cohort had a negative one. But both **climate and movement can track seasonal distance from the summer solstice**, including breeding, migration, resource access, tag operation and physiological state.

This new single, tightly frozen sensitivity asks: if every original source animal and every original near-equal-daylight date pair stays fixed, what happens to a **within-animal-year climate/GPS association after controlling LINEAR distance from the June 21 solstice**? It does NOT prove thermal causality or completely remove seasonality. Also, partialling out true seasonally accumulated biological effects can remove meaningful signal, not just confounding.

Within each unchanged marked animal and original Northern Hemisphere calendar year, original ascending and descending daylight dates straddle **June 21**. Define `k = 0.5 [(June21−rising_date).days + (falling_date−June21).days]` — the paired dates' average distance to the original solstice, reflecting the location on the annual calendar (and approximate daylength magnitude), not a covariate chosen by source response. For each original matched date pair, use the same first source ERA5 `ΔT` and NIGHT-minus-DAY original GPS displacement `Y`. The fixed linear partial slope was computed by separately regressing both `ΔT` and `Y` on intercept and `k` and regressing residual `Y` on residual `ΔT` (Frisch–Waugh–Lovell algebra). Separate DAY and NIGHT slopes were computed on the exact same pair roster and satisfy `βREL=βNIGHT−βDAY` at individual-year and cohort levels.

Source-year support was fixed **before new residual results**: >=10 original source date pairs, >=3 distinct k values, SD(k)>=7 days and SD(ΔT residualized on k)>0.5°C; for the separately-univariate water-balance exploratory slope require residual SD Δ30d(P−ET0)>10 mm, without controlling temperature simultaneously. None of these criteria was relaxed after results. For each original animal average its source-supported original calendar years equally, and weight each original animal once in cohort means. Report unadjusted slopes **on that exact same eligible subset** next to adjusted slopes to prevent selection effects being mistaken for calendar sensitivity. No event-level p-values, species-wide intervals or independent regional replicates were generated. Original mathematical test suite shows synthetic calendar-only X/Y correlations disappear after adjustment, while a source Y relation genuinely independent of k persists, with all undefined/collinear cases explicitly null.

**The black bear reversal is the most informative empirical diagnostic.** Its original source group mean thermal association, `−0.002903`, arose from **positive daytime βDAY=+0.003869** greater than positive night βNIGHT=+0.000966. Once original calendar-distance k is linearly controlled, its **daytime coefficient collapses to −0.000077** and the nighttime coefficient remains about **+0.001016**, resulting in a positive `βREL=+0.001094`. All **20 source animals** are still admitted; its leave-one-original-animal-out adjusted *cohort mean* is **[+0.000420,+0.001995]** (NOT statistical CI). This is **not** proof that black bears now have a true positive heat avoidance response: it shows the original negative regional temperature association **was not invariant to adjusting one explicit seasonal-calendar co-trend**.

**Mule deer** remain positive but attenuate from +0.003391 to +0.002103 across **all 118 originally qualified animals**; its DAY slope moves from **−0.002802** to **−0.000035**, and the NIGHT slope from **+0.000590** to **+0.002067**. Thus even a stable positive NIGHT-relative *group slope* can represent very different observed absolute DAY/NIGHT processes once seasonal-calendar structure is accounted for. The adjusted cohort mean leaves-one-original-animal-out range **[+0.002007,+0.002178]** is a sensitivity check, not animal population CI. **Virginia deer** remain positive: all **33 animals** retained, 32 positive adjusted individual source slopes; βDAY~−0.001035 and βNIGHT~+0.000567. Its original seasonal relative movement mean remained near zero (−0.00319), a separate question from climate co-trending.

**Water-balance predictor** (separate univariate, not a simultaneous causal climate model): Wyoming mule deer `βREL` on its source-admitted water-control individuals changed from **−0.072646** to **−0.015185 log1p(km/h) per 100mm modeled P−ET0**, with 117/118 original animals retaining source-qualified water calendar variation. In bears, the original water slope **+0.056566** became **+0.020901** among all 20 source animals. In the mule deer source, approximately **63%** of original paired-date water-proxy variance was linearly associated with k in source-supported animal-years (within-source mean of individual/year R²≈0.627); in black bears it was **43%** (R²≈0.429). Those R²s describe collinearity of a modeled covariate with seasonal k, NOT variance in animal movement that was caused by a calendar.

### What the sensitivity does and does NOT establish

**Supported observation:** The signed pattern of modeled air-temperature variation and relative GPS diel movement in original sampled populations is not simply an attribute of each taxon's name or a universal mean climate sign. Critically, previously reported source-level climate associations can **change sign** (black bear) or change their absolute DAY/NIGHT component interpretation (mule deer) after one scientifically motivated calendar-distance adjustment. Even with the same source recruitment cohort and GPS dates, unadjusted thermal slopes should NOT be elevated into a mechanism for nocturnal refuge, daytime suppression or adaptive clock memory.

**NOT supported:** a thermal regulatory causal effect, independent reproduction/forage stage control, endogenous photoperiod hysteresis, biome-general response predictions, random assignment of temperature, independent among-animal climate exposures, or an absence of temperature sensitivity when results change sign. Source-native GPS straight-line step displacement does not equal continuous activity/energy expenditure. The ERA5 temperature source is one fixed **coarse regional grid** per original cohort; individuals share annual temperature patterns. Original animal-mean signs are not independent meteorological experiments. k is linear only, and a calendar adjustment may also remove ecologically relevant variation that is genuinely tied to season.

This is explicitly exploratory after the six previous source results. No thresholds were revised, and no alternative k-polynomial, climate sampling window, species subset or source selection was searched. A real explanatory ecology paper needs **an independent environmental exposure/animal-population design**: original tagged animals monitored in overlapping years, co-located/animal-specific operative temperature and measured food/NDVI/water conditions, and independently held-out regions or species-specific replication. The observational six-source result cannot become a global thermal mechanism by adding more flexible adjustment terms.

### Ecological statement now defensible

> **At near-equal daylength, measured mammalian diel displacement differences and climate co-trends are context dependent; the apparent DAY-versus-NIGHT thermal pathway itself may change when a common seasonal calendar gradient is controlled.**

This is a useful diagnostic lesson for field seasonal ecology, not a claim that mammals collectively shift to night under heat. It complements earlier 21-point long-term modeled climate heterogeneity and Serengeti camera PHOTO-time/site NDVI, but **does not combine those different response measurements into a single numeric effect**.
