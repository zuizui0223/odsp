# Wetland is not one ecological treatment: a single Bukhansan camera dominates the Water-deer/Boar record contrast

**2026-10-15 | Post-publication ecological analysis | ODSP PR #264 (Draft)**

## Biological question

In Jeong, Shin & Kim (2025), Water Deer (*Hydropotes inermis*) and Wild Boar (*Sus scrofa*) were recorded at nine **forest** and two **wetland** cameras. Is the stronger pooled Water-deer representation in the wetland category a repeatable signal from the **two separate wetland cameras**, or is it mainly a concentration of source records at one particular location?

**Primary original field source:** Jeong et al. (2025), *Journal of Agriculture & Life Science* 59(5):111–118, DOI https://doi.org/10.14397/jals.2025.59.5.111, official [KCI publication](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART003260212). Original full-text Tables 1–2 publish both species' 11 camera-code × 24 clock-hour distributions. The ODSP v1 source contract retains only derived 3-bin site-level counts, all 11 public camera IDs, and independent consistency checks against all **24 author-published source clock-hour totals and the four original day/night species×habitat totals**. Source event counts: **184 Water Deer and 273 Wild Boar**, 457 jointly, from the 2019 June–September study.

These are **registered photographic records**; independent animals, camera-on hours, optical detection probability and species habitat availability were not verified. Camera sites were NOT randomized to forest or wetland. This is a secondary description of **where animals were detected**, not a test of preference, abundance, habitat suitability or causal behavior.

## Direct author-table camera contrasts

| Source camera category | Water Deer records | Wild Boar records | Water fraction among both species' records |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nine forest sites combined | 74 | 199 | 27.11% |
| Wetland camera **M1** | 18 | 25 | 41.86% |
| Wetland camera **M2** | **92** | **49** | **65.25%** |
| Both wetland cameras | **110** | **74** | **59.78%** |

Across the original whole camera network, Water Deer have **110/184 = 59.78%** of their source records in wetlands, while Wild Boar have **74/273 = 27.11%**; the difference of species-wise wetland record fractions is **+32.6764 percentage points**. The observed photograph count wetland-versus-forest *ratio of species record odds* is **(110×199)/(74×74) = 3.9974**. This is a source record ratio, NOT a real animal population habitat-selection odds ratio or a camera-effort-standardized rate.

**Crucial new ecological specificity:** Decompose the original species-wise wetland share difference *without inventing any camera*:

- **M2**: 92/184 − 49/273 = **+32.0513 percentage points** or **98.09% of the observed two-wetland-site contrast**.
- **M1**: 18/184 − 25/273 = **+0.6251 percentage points** or **1.91% of that contrast**.
- The two terms add EXACTLY to +32.6764 points.

This 98% figure is **specific to the original whole-network normalized record-share contrast**. It is **not** an allocation of causal habitat preference or a proof that M1 is ecologically irrelevant: M1 itself has a higher fraction of Water Deer among its photographed focal species than the pooled nine forest stations, but contributes relatively few total network records. Source station M2 alone contains **50% of all Water-deer registrations** and approximately **17.95% of the Wild-boar records**.

## Actual leave-one-wetland-camera-out calculations, with both denominators RECOMPUTED

| Original wetland source sites retained | Water wetland records / Water records in retained network | Boar wetland records / Boar records in retained network | Water−Boar wetland source-record share | Photograph record wetland/forest species OR |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| M1 + M2 (all 11 sites) | 110/184 | 74/273 | **+32.676 pp** | **3.997** |
| M1 ONLY (**M2 removed**; 10 sites) | 18/92 | 25/224 | **+8.405 pp** | **1.936** |
| M2 ONLY (**M1 removed**; 10 sites) | 92/166 | 49/248 | **+35.664 pp** | **5.049** |

The source-record association **does not reverse** after either omission, but its magnitude decreases strongly when M2 is absent. Neither reduced network tests replication of a wetland response: the remaining wetland is only **one physical camera**, not a repeated or randomized habitat treatment. The forest's 9 cameras are observational controls with potentially different effort and local vegetation.

## A simple exact spatial benchmark: where does this exact wetland pair rank?

Enumerate every one of the **55 unordered two-camera pairs** among the same **11 published camera codes**, keeping all original 184 Water and 273 Boar records and comparing Water-minus-Boar *shares of total species records* in those two camera sets. This is **not** a stochastic or experimental randomization of habitat: it ranks real source-camera record combinations.

- The actual **M1+M2 wetland pair ranks SECOND among all 55** pairs on the pre-specified descriptive record-share statistic, behind the hypothetical **forest G19 + wetland M2** pair.
- Only **2 of 55** observed station pairs have a contrast at least as large as M1+M2. The numerical fraction is **2/55 = 0.03636**, but **NOT a valid ecological permutation-test p-value**, because habitat labels were *not* randomized, cameras may be correlated, observation effort differs and the metric was explored after publication.
- This spatial ranking demonstrates that the two published wetland cameras form a high-contrast **observed photographic subset**, while the near-universal appearance of **M2** in the most contrasting pairs clarifies that the phenomenon is localized rather than proof of a general wetland mechanism.

For completeness, a separate formal **record-label-exchangeability** Fisher/hypergeometric calculation, fixing the number of photographs per habitat and overall Water/Boar record counts, yields an exact *idealized-photo-label* wetland one-sided tail **2.4986×10⁻¹²**, and for M2 versus other cameras **4.0816×10⁻¹³**. **Neither is an animal-population p-value**, because the 457 source photographs are not independently authenticated animal encounters, camera detection and operating exposure are unknown, and the comparisons are post-outcome. These record-level reference values **must not** be used to claim ecological selection or reproduce the original article's inferential claims.

## Integrating ecology and the exact temporal null: where versus when

The prior independently audited ODSP Bukhansan v4 source-route showed that the additional *time-binning* mismatch after conditioning on camera site (**0.036649944**) was **smaller** than its exact finite-record label allocation null expectation (**0.041139977**, formal one-sided reference probability **0.569788726301**). That was a **site-conditioned time** null, whereas today's site/habitat contrast considers **which camera records each species**, not the times of detection. They address different hypotheses, not one joint statistical test.

**Present ecological interpretation, with boundaries:**

1. There is a strong **observed local concentration of Water-deer registrations at M2** relative to the distribution of Wild-boar source records.
2. The difference should NOT be elevated to a general wetland habitat preference from only two camera sites, unequal photographic effort, unknown detection q and repeated animals.
3. There is still **no source-backed animal-level evidence** for a strong, repeatable within-camera temporal refuge or competition effect. The negative v4 clock-null result remains unchanged.
4. The next truly biological question is *what distinguishes M2 from M1 and well-sampled forest stations*: independent source camera operating time, vegetation/visibility, water availability and microhabitat, topography, trail/human activity by hour, repeat photo identities and season-specific movement could all matter. These mechanism variables **were not measured in this comparative analysis**, so none is claimed as the explanation.

The original source paper's nocturnal activity description concerns **daily sunrise/sunset classification**; our source-derived three-clock-bin comparisons do not reproduce dated astronomical phase. ODSP's original **Uljin 2022 fixed 41 daylength mirror-date pair** plan requires authentic NIE EcoBank version 1.1 dated events and independent station-hour ON/OFF exposure, still **SOURCE HOLD** issue #265. It must never be replaced or reclassified by this separate 2019 Bukhansan study.
