## Biological question sharper than pooled diel overlap

At the **same physical camera**, do Water deer registrations concentrate more in the author's printed clock rows 01:00–06:00 than in 19:00–24:00 **relative to Wild boar**? This is a deliberately exploratory **clock-label** contrast motivated by the article's reported Wild boar 19:00–21:00 and Water deer 02:00–04:00 peaks; **not** a confirmed solar-relative phase, causal disturbance response or user-predeclared 2022 Uljin mirror-date outcome.

The published 24 row labels are aggregated across multiple survey dates; each 6-label contrast has nominal equal time-bin count, but unknown exact bin endpoint semantics, operational gaps and q remain. The article does not supply independent station×hour camera ON/OFF logs, actual animal encounter or passage-distance exposures, or source timestamps. Never interpret the printed `24:00` row as a precisely verified clock interval or convert the table to true daily sunrise/sunset phases.

The source-authenticated checks are **24 printed total counts per species**, plus **species×forest/wetland exact counts** (above). Every manually derived station×three-window row is required to reproduce these published independent margins.

## Within the seven shared-observation cameras

- Of source-recorded 01–06 plus 19–24 detections at those **same seven sites**, Water deer have **74/158 = 46.84%** in 01–06, Boar **73/190 = 38.42%**. The observed within-shared-roster pooled difference is **+8.414 percentage points**, not a tested population behavior difference.
- A symmetric, noncausal **Kitagawa decomposition of THIS specific shared-roster photo-clock contrast** is **+10.220 pp** average within-camera detected-time profiles and **−1.806 pp** differing camera-photo weights = **+8.414 pp**. This is a different estimand from the prior forest-versus-wetland pooled *day/night* 55.68% habitat-photo-mix calculation; **there is no contradiction** because the time windows, site roster and weights differ. Both are descriptive arithmetic, not effects caused by habitat.
- Five of seven cameras have a higher 01–06 fraction for Water deer, two have a higher fraction for Boar. A simple site-direction two-sided binomial diagnostic is **p=0.453125** (which itself assumes independent sites, not established for animals in one park). Sites with only one Water-deer clock-window detection (G12 and G21) are especially unstable.
- A conventional Mantel–Haenszel common-site **event-count** morning/evening odds-ratio estimate is **1.6158**. The corresponding **unvalidated event-independence** chi-square diagnostic yields **p≈0.0466**. **DO NOT** publish the latter as evidence for a biologically replicated species effect; camera and animal dependence, selected site overlap, and source operation are unknown.
- **Remove just M2**: the same-site event-count OR falls to **1.3316**, with an event-level diagnostic **p≈0.3562**. Station M2 alone accounts for approximately **64%** of the sum of conditional Mantel–Haenszel score deviations. The positive detected-time signature is **sensitive to this one wetland camera**.
- In Forest-only cameras the exploratory site-stratified OR is **1.432**; in Wetland-only cameras **1.815**, but with only **two Wetland sites** these cannot establish a habitat interaction.

This is a meaningful improvement over the old pooled/night/day-only exercise: **published real camera IDs and species-specific record distributions are available**, and direct source co-occurrence support can be checked. But the key question “Does this represent repeatable independently supported biological chronotype partitioning?” is still **unresolved**.

## What is new and what was already in the authors' paper

The **authors already observed** late-evening Boar versus night/morning Water Deer peaks and published the underlying same-camera hour tables. We are not claiming those peaks as our discovery. Our additional secondary quantitative results are: source cross-checking and explicit **7/11 site sharing**, **M2 concentration**, the **same-site early/late contrast**, **site-direction support**, **one-camera leave-out sensitivity**, and a noncausal **within-site versus differing-camera-record-weight decomposition**. The exercise demonstrates both the potential of publicly printed ecological tables and the danger of treating a camera-hour pooled effect as independently replicated.

The original published `Δ4≈0.93` Boar and `≈0.92` Water Deer values compare **forest-versus-wetland activity curves within each species**, NOT an across-species Water Deer versus Wild Boar overlap. Avoid conflating them with Uljin's year-long cross-species Δ4.

## Evidence restrictions and next field research

Original study site×timestamp records and independent camera ON/OFF intervals were not provided, even though the printed site-hour counts are source-verified; cannot reconstruct mirror daylength dates, exact photoperiod, seasonal shifts, physical deployment uptime, individual identities or per-station detector q from printed counts. One camera may see repeated visits of the same individual. All 11 sites and zero-Water sites are retained descriptively, but requiring source-positive BOTH taxa for the site-level timing comparison is **outcome-conditioned**, suitable only for this retrospective description, not a pre-registered inference route.

To test an ecological mechanism convincingly, obtain repeated independent seasons and physical-station camera uptime, formalize shared species sampling support, use station-blocked/animal-aware observation process, and independently measure human timing and conditions. For ODSP's **existing frozen Uljin 41 date-pair route**, NIE EcoBank Version 1.1 source admission issue #265 remains separate and unsatisfied. No earlier ODSP result or user pre-data contract is reclassified.
